**Found and fixed on the way — fork-join children ran untraced.** The end-to-end test's Java-parity
assertion that each iteration's working step sees the inherited business cid failed with a random
UUID, while the single-delegation orchestrator test passes the same assertion. Telemetry probe
(`--nocapture`, trace ids per service): under the single delegation every child span shares the
parent's trace id; under the fan-out only the parent's four spans existed. All three fork-join sites
(`graph.extension`, `graph.task`, `graph.api.fetcher`) launched their batch on `tokio::spawn`ed
tasks, and the distributed-trace bracket is a tokio task-local, so the spawned child started outside
it: its `po.request` found no current trace, the launched flow or task ran untraced, and every
downstream call inside it fell back to a minted correlation id. A telemetry-presentation-parity
defect (`inv-telemetry-presentation-parity`) that the Increment-64 signature instrument never covered
because it had no fan-out shape. Fix: `common::join_batch` (`futures_util::future::join_all`; the
crate already rides in platform-core and sync-over-async) awaits the batch concurrently ON THE
CALLING TASK — Java's `po.request(batch)` shape — shared by the three skills. After the fix the probe
shows every child span (resume, task, counting step, suspend, store) under the parent's trace id for
both runs. Pinned by the end-to-end test's business-cid assertion, which is exactly the symptom.
Kept in this PR rather than split: it is one helper and three one-line call sites, the test that
found it is the lockstep's own, and shipping the index without it would ship a fan-out whose
suspensions are keyed correctly but invisible to the trace.
